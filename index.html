HOW TO READ THE RNA UNIVERSE

What this map
can tell you.

01 · IDENTITY

What kind of RNA is here?

Color by RNA class or family. A single-color island agrees with a known label; a mixed island may contain shared grammar or unresolved boundaries.

02 · EVIDENCE

How certain is the label?

Color or filter by evidence to separate direct Rfam matches, model-supported predictions, cluster propagation and computationally novel candidates.

03 · EVOLUTION

Where is it observed?

Color by taxonomy to distinguish domain-restricted neighborhoods from representation patterns shared across Bacteria, Archaea, Eukaryota or viruses.

04 · NEIGHBORHOOD

How coherent is the cluster?

Click a point to see its Leiden cluster size, Rfam anchor count and purity. High purity means known family labels agree locally.

05 · GRAMMAR

Which nucleotides matter?

Representative RNAs expose Layer-7/12 received attention and Head-20 zero-shot pairing scores at nucleotide resolution.

How the map was built

01

Represent

Layer-12 RiboGRAM representations summarize each RNA sequence. The pilot is length-filtered to 20–1,024 nt.

02

Connect

PCA-compressed embeddings form an approximate cosine k-nearest-neighbor graph, partitioned with Leiden.

03

Annotate

Direct RNAcentral/Rfam matches take precedence. High-confidence model and cluster-consensus labels are shown as predictions, never as curated facts.

04

Interrogate

Cluster representatives expose per-nucleotide Layer-7/12 received attention and zero-shot Head-20 pairing scores.

Interpretation boundary. UMAP axes have no biological unit and global distances can be distorted: read local neighborhoods, not left/right position. “Novel candidate” means computationally unannotated in this pipeline—not a claim of a new RNA family—and requires independent validation.
